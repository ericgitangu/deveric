# Context and problem

Workshop orders often arrive as handwritten lists or messages that must be re-entered, manually laid out, and priced by whoever is on shift. That makes quoting slow, creates avoidable transcription errors, and can waste material.

## Engineering approach

- **A customer-to-workshop workflow.** Each workshop can share an order link. Customers enter dimensions directly or upload a photo/PDF; image extraction creates draft rows that staff confirm before a job is created.
- **Plans that reflect the saw.** The optimizer accounts for blade kerf and grain direction, places cuts on available boards, and tracks reusable offcuts rather than treating every remainder as waste.
- **Quotes derived from the plan.** Material, cutting, and edging prices are applied to the same layout that produces the printable cutting plan, keeping the estimate and fulfilment instructions consistent.
- **Operational safeguards.** Idempotency keys make retries safe, workers claim jobs without duplicate processing, invoice numbers are allocated transactionally, and uploads are validated by file content.
- **Human control at the boundary.** AI assists with reading documents but does not approve a quote or create a priced job without staff confirmation.

## Outcomes and trade-offs

The prototype connects intake, optimization, quoting, and job follow-up in one workflow, replacing manual re-entry with a repeatable process. Sample data is used; payment processing remains in a sandbox and the production deposit flow is not enabled. The key trade-off is deliberate: uncertain extraction is surfaced for review rather than being allowed to silently change a cut list or price.

## Stack

- Next.js 15, React 19, and strict TypeScript
- Supabase PostgreSQL, row-level security, and Supabase Storage
- Zod validation, PDF generation, and image-assisted extraction
- Vitest and PostgreSQL integration tests
- Vercel deployment
