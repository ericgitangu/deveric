# Context and problem

Mature service platforms accumulate operational risk across application code, infrastructure, data access, deployment, and observability. A useful audit must connect findings to evidence and to a practical remediation path rather than produce a disconnected list of recommendations.

## Engineering approach

- **Evidence before conclusions.** Findings are linked to reproducible checks, architecture records, and acceptance criteria so that reviewers can distinguish verified behavior from assumptions.
- **Workstream-based assessment.** The review covers application boundaries, data integrity, access controls, release safety, observability, capacity, and operational readiness.
- **Staged remediation.** Recommendations are organized by dependency and risk, with decision records documenting trade-offs and explicit conditions for production changes.
- **Reviewable hand-off.** A structured evidence set and walkthrough make results usable by engineering, operations, and leadership without requiring each audience to reconstruct the investigation.

## Outcomes and trade-offs

The audit turns a broad platform review into a traceable readiness program with a clear separation between observed evidence, proposed work, and decisions that require an owner. Sensitive environment details and exploit-level findings are intentionally excluded from this public summary.

## Stack

- Legacy PHP service architecture and MongoDB
- Redis, Nginx, and containerized staging workflows
- Structured tests, operational metrics, and architecture decision records
- Security, data-integrity, deployment, and production-readiness review methods
